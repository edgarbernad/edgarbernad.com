Applied linguistics · Multimodality · Science communication

Meaning is made beyond words.

I'm Edgar Bernad-Mechó, an applied linguist at Universitat Jaume I in Castelló, Spain, and a member of the GRAPE research group. I study how gesture, gaze, images, editing and sound work together with language.

Much of my recent work looks at science communication on social media: how researchers and creators use Instagram reels, YouTube videos and TED talks to make specialised knowledge engaging for wide audiences. I also study university lectures and English-medium instruction, and what all of this means for multimodal literacy: how students and teachers learn to read and create meaning across words, images, sound and video.

Research

Three lines, one question

When we teach, present or explain, how much of the meaning travels outside the words? I have followed that question from the lecture hall to the screen.

Digital genres

Science dissemination on screen

How do TED talks, YouTube videos, Instagram reels and TV series turn specialised knowledge into something people want to watch?

I analyse how creators recontextualise science and engage their audiences multimodally, from modal density and coherence to humour and filmic choices.

Start here Multimodal engagement strategies in science disseminationDiscourse Studies, 2023 Modal density and coherence in science disseminationJournal of English for Academic Purposes, 2022
Lectures & EMI

Multimodal metadiscourse in university lectures

How do lecturers organise and signpost their talk with gesture, gaze, movement and voice?

This line began with my PhD on organisational metadiscourse in lectures. It now looks at English-medium instruction and intercultural classrooms, and feeds directly into training for lecturers who teach in English.

Start here Building successful communication in EMI contextsChinese Journal of Applied Linguistics, 2024 Combining multimodal techniques to study spoken academic lecturesAtlantis, 2021
Multimodal literacy

From analysis to the classroom

What should students and teachers learn about meaning-making in digital genres, and how can AI help them compose?

I turn descriptive findings into teaching: multimodal literacy in English for specific and academic purposes, digital multimodal composing, and teacher education.

Research software

GRAPE-MARS

Multimodal Analysis Research Software is an open-source tool for annotating and analysing video, developed with my colleagues at GRAPE and registered at Universitat Jaume I in 2023.

It brings together automatic transcription with AI, layered annotation tiers, and co-occurrence analysis that turns annotation into results you can explore and export. We have presented it in Spain, the Netherlands, Malaysia, Japan, the United Kingdom, Singapore and Taiwan.

Coming in the next months

We are building a more powerful second version, with AI-assisted annotation and in-depth analysis.

I am happy to run workshops on GRAPE-MARS and video-based multimodal analysis for research groups and postgraduate programmes.
